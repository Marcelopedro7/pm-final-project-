# Problem Hook & Value Proposition (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own):** RouteLogic
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** Customers may churn as competitors offer more efficient and flexible route-planning solutions.
- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** The user is forced to manually adjust routes using spreadsheets, mapping tools, and phone calls to coordinate changes
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** Customers may churn as competitors offer more efficient solutions, while operations teams are forced to rely on spreadsheets, mapping tools, and phone calls to manage route changes.
- **Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** We will allow operations teams to pre-establish and optimize routes, while continuing to manage them offline when the main system is unavailable, reducing manual work and operational disruption.

## Cold-read your own hook
- **Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:** High — losing customers to competitors could directly impact revenue and market position.
- **Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?:** Systemic — operations teams regularly need to adjust routes and coordinate changes, making manual work a recurring problem.
- **Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?:** Yes — directly reduces manual route management by allowing teams to pre-establish and optimize routes and continue managing them offline when the main system is unavailable.
